# Asset allocation and diversification

## Why spread money across assets
Different assets behave differently. Equity has higher long-term growth potential but can fall sharply; fixed deposits and the Public Provident Fund (PPF) are steadier but grow more slowly; gold often behaves differently from both. Holding a mix reduces the impact of any single asset doing badly.

## Common asset types in India
- Fixed deposits: predictable interest and low risk; the interest is taxable.
- PPF: a government-backed long-term savings scheme with a 15-year lock-in and an interest rate set by the government each quarter.
- Equity mutual funds and stocks: market-linked, higher risk, suited to long horizons.
- Gold, including sovereign gold bonds and gold ETFs: often used as a diversifier.
- Debt mutual funds: invest in bonds; risk depends on the fund type.

## Matching risk to goals
Money for a goal less than three years away is usually kept in low-risk instruments. Long-term goals can take more risk because there is time to recover from market falls. Your comfort with seeing values drop also matters: a plan you abandon in a downturn is worse than a more conservative plan you keep.

## Diversification is not a guarantee
Diversification reduces risk; it does not remove it. This information is educational and is not a recommendation to buy any particular product.
