# Budgeting and the 50/30/20 rule

## What a budget is for
A budget is a plan for where each month's income goes before it is spent. It does not have to be strict; its job is to make trade-offs visible. People who track spending for even a few months usually discover categories that quietly grew, such as food delivery or subscriptions.

## The 50/30/20 rule
The 50/30/20 rule is a simple starting point for splitting take-home income:
- about 50% for needs: rent, groceries, utilities, transport, insurance, school fees and minimum loan payments;
- about 30% for wants: dining out, shopping, entertainment, travel and hobbies;
- about 20% for savings and investments, including building an emergency fund and paying down debt faster.

It is a guideline, not a law. In a high-rent city, needs may take 60% at first; the useful question is whether savings can still reach 20% over time.

## Needs versus wants
A need is something you would still pay for if your income fell sharply. A want is optional or could be cheaper. The same category can contain both: groceries are a need, but premium food delivery is mostly a want. FinTwin labels Groceries as essential and Food & Dining as discretionary for this reason.

## Making a budget stick
Automate savings on salary day so saving happens first, not with whatever is left at month end. Review the largest discretionary categories monthly. Set a limit for the one or two categories that grow fastest instead of trying to control everything at once.
