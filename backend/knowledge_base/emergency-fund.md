# Emergency funds

## What an emergency fund is
An emergency fund is money set aside only for genuine surprises: job loss, a medical bill not fully covered by insurance, urgent home or vehicle repairs, or helping family in a crisis. It is not for planned purchases or holidays.

## How big it should be
A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses. Essential expenses include rent, groceries, utilities, transport, insurance premiums and loan EMIs, because EMIs must still be paid during an emergency. Aim for the higher end if you are the only earner in the family, have dependents, are self-employed, or work in an industry with unstable jobs.

## Where to keep it
The money should be safe and quickly accessible: a savings account, a sweep-in fixed deposit or a liquid fund. Avoid keeping it in assets whose value can fall sharply, such as equity, because emergencies often coincide with market falls.

## Building it step by step
Start with a first target of one month of essentials, then grow it to three and later six months. Directing a fixed amount every month works better than waiting for a large surplus. If you already hold idle cash in your bank account, simply earmarking part of it as the emergency fund can close the gap immediately.

## Using it and refilling it
Use it without guilt when a real emergency happens; that is its purpose. Afterwards, pause other discretionary goals and refill it first.
