# Savings ratio and cash flow

## Savings ratio
The savings ratio is the share of income left after spending and loan EMIs. Money that goes into investments counts as saved. A ratio of 20% or more is a widely used benchmark; below 10% leaves little room to build wealth or absorb surprises.

## Cash flow
Monthly cash flow is income minus everything that leaves your account: spending, EMIs and investment contributions. Positive cash flow means your balance grows; negative cash flow means you are drawing on savings. One negative month caused by a planned purchase is normal. Repeated negative months usually mean spending has outgrown income.

## Stability matters
Steady months are easier to plan around than volatile ones. Large irregular expenses, such as annual insurance premiums, festival shopping or travel, are predictable if you plan for them. Setting aside a small amount each month for known annual costs turns a spike into a smooth monthly cost.

## Lifestyle creep
When income rises, spending often rises with it without a conscious decision. This lifestyle creep is the most common reason savings stay flat despite pay raises. Deciding in advance to save at least half of every raise is a simple defence.

## Improving savings
Start with the largest discretionary categories, not the smallest. Cancel unused subscriptions, set a limit for food delivery and shopping, and automate a transfer to savings on salary day.
