# Planning for financial goals

## Make goals specific
A useful goal has an amount, a date and a priority, for example: buy a car for ₹8 lakh in two years. Vague goals such as "save more" are hard to track.

## How much to save each month
Without investment returns, the monthly saving needed is the remaining amount divided by the months left. For example, a ₹8 lakh car with ₹2 lakh already saved and 24 months to go needs ₹25,000 a month. With returns, the required saving is lower; the annuity formula P = (FV − PV × (1 + i)^n) × i / ((1 + i)^n − 1) gives the exact amount for a monthly return i.

## Priorities when money is limited
When the monthly surplus cannot fund every goal, fund them in order of priority. Usually an emergency fund comes first, then goals with fixed deadlines, then flexible ones. Planning more than you can actually save only creates the illusion of progress.

## Closing a gap
If a goal is off track there are four levers: save more each month, free up spending to raise the surplus, move the target date later, or reduce the target amount. Comparing the effect of each makes the trade-off clear.

## Time horizon and risk
Short-term goals should be kept in safe instruments so that the money is there when needed. Longer-term goals can use investments with higher expected returns, accepting more ups and downs along the way.
