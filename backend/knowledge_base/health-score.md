# Understanding the FinTwin Financial Health Score

## What the score measures
The Financial Health Score summarises financial health on a scale of 0 to 100. It is a weighted average of seven components, each based on a measurable indicator and a widely used personal-finance guideline.

## The seven components
- Savings (20%): the savings ratio; 20% or more is the benchmark.
- Emergency fund (20%): months of essential expenses and EMIs covered; 3 to 6 months is the guideline.
- Debt burden (20%): EMIs as a share of income; lower is better, above 36% is high.
- Cash-flow stability (15%): how many recent months had positive cash flow and how much spending varies.
- Investments (10%): the share of income invested, and its spread across asset types.
- Goal progress (10%): how much of each goal's required saving the monthly surplus can fund.
- Spending behaviour (5%): discretionary spending as a share of income, plus recent unusual transactions.

## Grades
A is 85 and above (excellent), B is 70 to 84 (good), C is 55 to 69 (fair), D is 40 to 54 (weak) and E is below 40 (critical).

## Missing data
If a component cannot be measured, for example when no goals have been set, it is left out and the other weights are scaled up, rather than counting it as zero.

## Using the score
The score is a summary, not a judgement. The components and recommendations show which changes would raise it most.
