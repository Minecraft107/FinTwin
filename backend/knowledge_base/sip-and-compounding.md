# SIPs and compounding

## What a SIP is
A systematic investment plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually monthly, into a mutual fund. It builds discipline because the investment happens automatically, without needing to decide each month.

## Rupee-cost averaging
Because a SIP invests the same amount every month, it buys more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high. This rupee-cost averaging reduces the risk of investing a large sum at a bad time. It does not guarantee a profit or protect against losses in a falling market.

## Compounding
Compounding means returns themselves earn returns. Its effect grows with time, which is why starting early matters more than starting big. The future value of regular monthly investments is FV = P × ((1 + r)^n − 1) / r, where P is the monthly amount, r the monthly return and n the number of months.

## Returns are not guaranteed
Equity mutual funds can fall in value, especially over short periods. Long-term averages are not promises. Match the investment to the time horizon: money needed within a few years is usually better kept in lower-risk options.
