# Debt, EMIs and debt-to-income

## How an EMI works
An equated monthly instalment (EMI) repays a loan in equal monthly amounts. The formula is EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1), where P is the principal, r the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12) and n the number of months. Early EMIs are mostly interest; later EMIs are mostly principal.

## Debt-to-income ratio
The debt-to-income ratio is total monthly EMIs divided by monthly take-home income. Personal-finance guidelines commonly suggest keeping it below about 20–30%, and treating anything above roughly 36% as high. Many Indian lenders assess a similar measure called FOIR (fixed obligations to income ratio) and are reluctant to lend when it approaches 50%.

## Good and expensive debt
Debt that builds a long-term asset at a moderate rate, such as a home loan or an education loan, is usually easier to justify than high-interest consumer debt. Credit-card balances that are not paid in full can cost 36–42% a year, so clearing them is usually the first priority.

## Paying debt down faster
Two common methods are the avalanche method (extra payments to the highest-interest loan first, which saves the most interest) and the snowball method (smallest balance first, which builds motivation). Before prepaying, check for prepayment charges and keep your emergency fund intact.

## Before taking a new loan
Check the EMI against your monthly surplus, not just your income. A new EMI that consumes most of your surplus leaves no room for savings or surprises. Simulating the loan first shows its effect on cash flow, the emergency fund and your goals.
