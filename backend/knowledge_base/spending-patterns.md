# Spending patterns, subscriptions and unusual charges

## Category drift
Spending in a category can rise slowly month after month, for example food delivery or online shopping. Comparing the last three months with the three before shows whether a category is drifting up. Catching drift early is easier than cutting back later.

## Subscriptions
Streaming services, apps, gym memberships and software plans are small individually but add up, and they renew automatically. Reviewing subscriptions every few months and cancelling unused ones is one of the easiest savings.

## Festive and seasonal spending
Spending often rises during festivals such as Diwali, during travel season and when annual bills fall due. These spikes are predictable; planning for them avoids negative months.

## Unusual or duplicate charges
A transaction that is far larger than your usual spending in that category deserves a second look, as does the same amount charged twice on the same day by the same merchant. Most are legitimate, but duplicates and errors do happen, and banks and merchants can reverse them if reported promptly.

## Reducing spending without hurting
Focus on the biggest discretionary categories first, set a monthly limit rather than banning things outright, and redirect the money saved to a goal so the saving feels purposeful.
