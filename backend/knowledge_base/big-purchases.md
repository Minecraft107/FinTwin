# Big purchases: affordability and timing

## Can I afford it?
A purchase is comfortably affordable when it can be paid without touching the emergency fund, without leaving the account below about one month of outgoings, and without delaying important goals. Being able to pay is not the same as being able to afford it.

## Saving first versus borrowing
Saving for a purchase over a few months avoids interest and keeps cash flow unchanged. Financing spreads the cost but adds interest and a new EMI that reduces the monthly surplus for the loan's full term. Zero-cost EMI offers can still include processing fees or a higher price.

## Opportunity cost
Money spent on a purchase is money not available for goals or investments. A large purchase may delay goals even if it is affordable today.

## A simple check
Compare the price with your monthly surplus: the number of months of surplus it represents is a quick measure of how big the purchase really is for you. Simulating the purchase shows its effect on cash, the emergency fund, the health score and goals before you commit.

## Planned versus impulse
Waiting a few days before a large discretionary purchase reduces regret. Planned purchases fit into a budget; impulse purchases usually come out of savings.
